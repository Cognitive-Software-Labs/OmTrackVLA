Ridgeback · lab project

Follow one person through a crowd

A Clearpath Ridgeback locks onto the person in front of it and follows them through a busy space. It keeps the right person when others walk between, look alike or block the way, and it stops instead of guessing when it is unsure.

The problem

Three things go wrong in crowds

Someone walks between

For a moment the camera cannot see the leader at all. The robot has to remember who it was following and pick them up again afterwards.

Someone looks the same

Two dark jackets look nearly identical from behind. Colour alone cannot tell them apart; where each person has been moving can.

People are in the way

The shortest path to the leader runs through other people. The robot must keep a safe gap to everyone, not only to the person it follows.

What the robot has

Lidar sees everyone. The camera sees who they are.

Two lidars, 270° each

One at the front, one at the rear: together a full circle. They measure where every person is, many times a second, in the dark too. They only see outlines, so they cannot tell people apart.

One front camera

Shows clothing and appearance, which is what identifies the leader. It only looks forward, and people block it.

A base that moves sideways

The Ridgeback drives in any direction and turns on the spot, so it can keep the camera on the leader while it side-steps around people.

So the plan combines them: lidar keeps track of where each person goes, and the camera confirms which of them is the leader.

Keeping the right person

The lock never jumps to someone else

If the robot is not sure it sees the leader, it stops and looks. It never follows a best guess.

Following the wrong person quietly is the worst failure, worse than stopping. So the lock only moves the robot when the leader is confirmed, and a new person has to pass two independent checks before the lock accepts them as the leader again.

Each person crop becomes an appearance fingerprint. A network trained once on thousands of other people puts photos of the same person close together. At lock-on the robot stores a few snapshots of the leader; nobody is trained per person.

Why build this first

A baseline before the big model

The repository started as a deployment of OmTrackVLA, a vision-language-action model that turns camera images and a sentence into a path. It stays as a candidate. A simple, measurable system comes first, so the model has something to beat.

How we will know

Same scenarios, same scores, for every approach

During tests the leader wears a marker the robot never uses. It records where the leader really was, so every mistake can be counted.

How crowded?

A lab corridor with a few people, or a busy public hall? This sets the test densities and speed limits.

One camera enough?

With only a front camera, identity is confirmed only ahead of the robot. The lidar has to carry the rest.

Lidar height?

Lidars at shin height see legs; higher ones see bodies. That decides which people detector fits.
